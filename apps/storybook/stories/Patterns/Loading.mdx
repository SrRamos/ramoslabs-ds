  <h1>Show the shape of what is coming, or show nothing</h1>
  <p>A loading state is a promise about what arrives next. The skeleton is one of three tools, and most loading problems come from reaching for the wrong one.</p>

    <h2 className="skl-section-title">Skeleton, spinner, or button busy state</h2>
  </div>
  <p className="skl-section-text">Three indicators answer three different questions. Ask them in order. Did the user just press something? Is a region or page loading with a known layout? Is the wait short, open ended, or very long? The first yes picks the tool.</p>

    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>

    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> a skeleton is a promise about structure.       <tr><th scope="row">After 800ms</th><td>Skeleton from 300ms, content the moment data arrives.</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Do not show a skeleton for a load that ends within 300ms, <code>--motion-skeleton-delay</code>.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> under about a second the user's flow of thought is intact, and NN/g notes a flashing placeholder makes a fast page harder to follow. Showing nothing is the fastest-feeling answer.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"Response at 180ms: content appears, no skeleton was ever painted"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">The user sees one state change, from the old view to the new content.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"Skeleton painted for 180ms, then replaced"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">The placeholder flashes in and out, which reads as flicker and costs attention for no information.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Once a skeleton is visible, keep it at least 500ms, <code>--motion-skeleton-min-duration</code>.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> content that arrives just after the delay would show the placeholder for a few frames and remove it. A minimum display time turns that flash into a readable beat. The cost is up to 500ms of extra wait in that narrow band, accepted on purpose.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"Response at 320ms: skeleton from 300ms, content at 800ms"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">The skeleton lasts long enough to register and the swap is clean.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"Remove the skeleton the moment data arrives, whatever the elapsed time"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">A placeholder visible for 20ms is a flash, not a state.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Read both values from the tokens and leave the defaults alone unless a flow has a measured reason.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> one pair of numbers keeps every surface in the system feeling the same, and the tokens carry the sourcing in their descriptions.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"<SJSkeleton shape=\"group\" :loading=\"pending\">"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">The group reads <code>--motion-skeleton-delay</code> and <code>--motion-skeleton-min-duration</code> for you.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"<SJSkeleton shape=\"group\" :delay=\"150\" :min-duration=\"2000\">"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">Hand-tuned numbers per screen drift apart, and a long minimum makes fast data feel slow.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

<div className="skl-section">
  <div className="skl-section-header">
    <span className="skl-section-number">4</span>
    <h2 className="skl-section-title">Shape mirrors the layout and reserves its space</h2>
  </div>
  <p className="skl-section-text">The point of a skeleton is that the swap moves nothing. web.dev calls a Cumulative Layout Shift of 0.1 or less good; the target here is 0 for the swap. The demo below renders the placeholder and the loaded content at identical sizes.</p>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Make the placeholder the same size as the content it stands in for, so the swap causes no layout shift.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> web.dev says to reserve sufficient space in advance with a placeholder or skeleton, and that removing reserved space "can cause just as much CLS as inserting content." The group reserves the space even during the 300ms delay.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"text lines = expected lines; circle size = avatar size; rect ratio = media ratio"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">Three lines in, three lines out. The heading below does not move.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"One tall block for a three row list, or no placeholder until 300ms"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">The content lands at a different height and everything under it jumps.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Build the placeholder from the shape primitives: text lines, a circle for avatars, a rect with a ratio for media.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> NN/g recommends skeletons whose gray boxes match the text and images of the final layout and warns against frame-only skeletons that show nothing about structure. Polaris matches line count to the content.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"<SJSkeleton shape=\"rect\" ratio=\"16/9\" />"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">The ratio reserves the media height from the width before the image loads, the same idea as CSS <code>aspect-ratio</code>.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"A header, a footer, and an empty middle"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">A frame-only skeleton gives no sense of structure and can look like the page is broken.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Keep static content real and skeleton only the parts that load.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> Polaris says to keep fixed elements, such as a title that never changes, as real content, and not to use placeholder content for titles that will change. A fake title is something the user must unlearn.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"Real \"Recent activity\" heading + skeleton rows"}</code>

    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"Skeleton for a heading that is always the same"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">It delays something you already know and adds shimmer for no reason.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

<div className="skl-section">
  <div className="skl-section-header">
    <span className="skl-section-number">5</span>
    <h2 className="skl-section-title">Motion, forced colors, and direction</h2>
  </div>
  <p className="skl-section-text">The shimmer is a hint, not a feature. It signals that work is going on, it is slow (<code>--motion-skeleton-shimmer</code>, 1600ms per pass), and it has three fallbacks.</p>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Use a slow, subtle shimmer and make it static under <code>prefers-reduced-motion</code>.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> NN/g says animation signals activity but should stay subtle because it can distract or cause accessibility problems. MDN: the reduce setting asks for motion to be removed, reduced, or replaced. Under reduce the placeholder keeps its flat base fill and still reads as a placeholder.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { shimmer off }"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">The shape and the announcement carry the meaning, so nothing is lost when the sweep stops.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"A fast pulse or bounce, or a shimmer that ignores the preference"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">Fast or large motion is a vestibular trigger, and an always-on loop is the content WCAG 2.2.2 says needs a stop control.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Under <code>forced-colors: active</code>, draw a border and drop the gradient.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> MDN: in forced colors mode author background colors are ignored, non-url background images become none, and box-shadow becomes none. A fill-only placeholder vanishes. A border in a system color survives.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"@media (forced-colors: active) { border: 1px solid CanvasText }"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">The outline of each block is still drawn in the user's own palette.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"Fill or shadow as the only way a block is visible"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">In Windows High Contrast the user sees an empty page with no sign anything is loading.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Flip the shimmer direction in right-to-left layouts.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> a sweep should travel the way the user reads. Logical properties flip layout for you, but there is no logical property for an animation, so the sweep is mirrored explicitly.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"Sweep runs inline-start to inline-end (right to left in RTL)"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">The motion agrees with the reading direction in Arabic and Hebrew, as the layout around it does.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"A hard-coded `translateX(100%)` that always travels left to right"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">In RTL it moves against the text, which reads as a mistake.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div className="skl-section">
  <div className="skl-section-header">
    <span className="skl-section-number">6</span>
    <h2 className="skl-section-title">Accessibility: hide the shapes, announce once</h2>
  </div>
  <p className="skl-section-text">A skeleton is a picture of loading, and a screen reader cannot use a picture. Support for <code>aria-busy</code> is uneven (Roselli found only JAWS honored it in 2020, not re-tested here), so the semantics are set but the announcement never depends on them.</p>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Set <code>aria-busy="true"</code> on the container and <code>aria-hidden="true"</code> on the placeholder blocks.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> MDN: <code>aria-busy</code> tells assistive tech the region is being modified and that it may wait before announcing, and <code>aria-hidden</code> removes decorative content from the accessibility tree. Without the hidden blocks a screen reader can walk through empty boxes. Never hide a focusable element.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"<div aria-busy=\"true\"> <span aria-hidden=\"true\"> ...shapes... </span> </div>"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">Semantics on the region, silence on the shapes, and the group sets both for you.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"`aria-busy` alone, as the only signal"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">Most screen readers ignore it, so the user hears nothing or hears empty placeholders.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Announce once through a polite status message when the skeleton becomes visible, and never ship a skeleton with no text announcement.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> WCAG 4.1.3 requires status messages to be exposed without moving focus. A silent skeleton tells a screen reader user nothing is happening. Use <code>announce</code>, which writes to a polite live region, not an assertive one, once, when the skeleton becomes visible.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"<SJSkeleton shape=\"group\" announce=\"Loading activity\">"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">One short sentence, spoken once, in the user's idle moment.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"`role=\"alert\"` or assertive on a skeleton, or one announcement per block"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">It interrupts, repeats, and shouts about something that is not an emergency.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Inside <code>SJState</code> with <code>variant="loading"</code>, leave <code>announce</code> off the inner group.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> the state already carries the message that tells the user what is loading. A second live region speaks the same thing twice. The skeleton slot is visual only.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"<SJState variant=\"loading\"> <template #skeleton>...</template> </SJState>"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">One layer speaks, one layer draws.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"A skeleton group with `announce` inside an SJState that already announces"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">The user hears "Loading" twice in a row.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div className="skl-section">
  <div className="skl-section-header">
    <span className="skl-section-number">7</span>
    <h2 className="skl-section-title">What a skeleton is not for</h2>
  </div>
  <p className="skl-section-text">A skeleton means content is on the way. If nothing is on the way, or the wait has gone wrong, it says the wrong thing.</p>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Do not use a skeleton for actions, errors, or empty states. Use the button busy state, or <code>SJState</code>.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> Carbon says action components such as buttons and inputs usually do not need skeletons and that toasts, menus, and modals do not get them. An error or an empty result needs words and a next step, which a gray shape cannot give.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"Load failed: SJState variant=\"error\" with Retry. Zero rows: SJState variant=\"empty\""}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">The user learns what happened and what to do.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"A skeleton left shimmering after the request failed, or as the \"empty\" view"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">It reads as forever loading, with no way out and no explanation.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Time-box the skeleton: when a load runs long, switch to progress or an error.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> Carbon says skeletons "should only appear for only a few seconds." A looping shimmer that outlasts the wait invites the question whether the page is broken, and WCAG 2.2.2 expects auto-starting motion beside other content to be stoppable after 5 seconds.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"After a long wait, swap to progress with an estimate, or to an error with Retry"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">The state follows what is actually happening.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"A 30 second shimmer with no timeout"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">The user cannot tell slow from stuck.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div className="skl-section">
  <div className="skl-section-header">
    <span className="skl-section-number">8</span>
    <h2 className="skl-section-title">Restraint: one level, few blocks</h2>
  </div>
  <p className="skl-section-text">More shimmer is not more helpful. A calm screen shows the structure and keeps motion low.</p>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Use one level of skeleton per region and keep the number of independently shimmering blocks small.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> Fluent 2 keeps simultaneous skeleton animations in sync so the page does not feel fragmented, and nesting a placeholder in a placeholder adds motion without adding information. The block count is judgment, not research: group rows into one region and let <code>text</code> with <code>lines</code> stand for a paragraph.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"One group per region; `text` with `lines=\"3\"` for a paragraph"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">A few calm blocks that read as the page layout.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"Nested groups, or forty independent rows each shimmering on its own clock"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">Visual noise that competes with the content that is about to arrive.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

  <div className="skl-section-header">
    <span className="skl-section-number">9</span>
    <h2 className="skl-section-title">Contrast: the fill is decorative</h2>
  </div>
  <p className="skl-section-text">The placeholder fill, <code>--skeleton-color-base</code>, is deliberately low contrast: slate 200 on white is 1.23:1, and the highlight on the base is 1.13:1 (computed with the WCAG relative-luminance formula). This is a decision with a counter-argument, and both are stated here.</p>

  <div className="skl-rule">
    <div className="skl-rule-label"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">gavel</span>Rule</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-text">Treat the placeholder fill as decorative: do not raise it to the 3:1 non-text floor, and carry the meaning in text and in the forced-colors border.</div>
    <div className="skl-rule-why"><strong>Why:</strong> WCAG 1.4.11 excludes graphics "for aesthetic purposes that does not require the user to see or understand it." The shapes are hidden from assistive tech, the announcement carries the loading state, and in forced colors each block gets a <code>CanvasText</code> border. Roselli argues skeletons convey state and so fail 1.4.11. W3C does not address skeletons, so the decorative reading is an interpretation. A louder fill would compete with the content it stands in for.</div>
  </div>

  <div className="skl-ra">
    <div className="skl-ra-card rec">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">check_circle</span>✓ Recommended</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"Hidden shapes + announced state + forced-colors border"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">No meaning depends on the fill, so nothing depends on its contrast.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="skl-ra-card avoid">
      <div className="skl-ra-head"><span className="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cancel</span>✕ Avoid</div>
      <code className="skl-ra-code">{"Letting the placeholder be the only place a status or a label appears"}</code>
      <p className="skl-ra-note">If a block carries meaning, it is content, not a placeholder, and it must pass 4.5:1 or 3:1 as content does.</p>
